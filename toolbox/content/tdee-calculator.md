## How it works

Total daily energy expenditure is the energy a body uses across a whole day, resting and moving
together. The calculator reaches it in two steps, and it shows both.

First it estimates the resting rate with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, from weight in kilograms,
height in centimeters and age in years:

`BMR = 10 * weight + 6.25 * height - 5 * age + 5`

The constant is `+ 5` for male and `- 161` for female. Then it multiplies that estimate by the
activity factor you choose:

`maintenance calories = BMR * activity multiplier`

1. Choose metric or imperial units.
2. Enter your height, your weight and your age.
3. Choose the sex used by the formula.
4. Choose one of the five activity levels.
5. Read the daily figure, the resting rate behind it, and the factor applied.

The five factors are the only ones the tool accepts. **Sedentary** is 1.2, for little or no planned
exercise. **Lightly active** is 1.375, for light exercise 1 to 3 days each week. **Moderately
active** is 1.55, for moderate exercise 3 to 5 days each week. **Very active** is 1.725, for hard
exercise 6 to 7 days each week. **Extra active** is 1.9, for very hard exercise or a physical job.

The result names the level and the number, as `Moderately active × 1.55`, so the assumption behind
the figure stays visible.

## Worked examples

### A man of 30, 175 cm and 70 kg, moderately active

The resting rate is 1,649 kcal/day and the daily figure is **2,556 kcal/day**.

The same man across all five levels gives 1,979, then 2,267, then 2,556, then 2,844, then 3,133.
Each step up adds about 289 kilocalories, and the whole range spans 1,154 a day.

### A woman of 35, 162 cm and 58 kg, lightly active

The resting rate is 1,257 kcal/day and the daily figure is **1,728 kcal/day**. At very active, the
same measurements give 2,167.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which activity level should I choose?

The tool lists five and describes each one, and it does not choose for you. The description is the
whole definition, so read it rather than the label. One step in either direction is worth about 289
kilocalories a day in the first example.

### What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

The resting rate covers a body at rest and nothing else. This figure covers the day around it, and
it is that resting rate multiplied by one factor. For the resting rate on its own, use the
[BMR Calculator](/bmr-calculator).

### Is the activity factor measured?

No. It is a broad estimate, and the tool says so with the result. Two people who pick the same
level can spend very different amounts of energy, because a factor cannot see how long you train,
how hard, or how much you move for the rest of the day.

### Why does my fitness tracker give a different number?

A tracker builds its figure from movement it detects and a model of its own. This tool applies one
factor you chose to one equation. The two methods rarely agree.

### Does this tell me how much to eat?

No. It prints an estimate of the energy a day costs, and nothing beyond it. There is no target
here, no deficit, no surplus and no plan. A doctor or a registered dietitian can tell you what to
do with the number.

### Is anything I type stored?

No. There is no account and no server call. Nothing you type leaves the browser, nothing is saved,
and every field returns to its default when you reload the page. The tool also works with no
internet connection after your first visit.

## Good to know

The multiplier scales the whole resting estimate, and the error in it too. That resting rate is a
population average for your four numbers, and the day figure inherits every way you differ from it.

Nothing in this calculation is measured. It is one published equation and one factor from a short
list, applied to numbers you typed.

hand, use the [Calculator](/calculator).
